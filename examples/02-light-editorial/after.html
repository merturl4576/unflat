“I don’t design objects. I design the moment you stop noticing them.”
Tomás Reyes, cabinetmaker, Lisbon
What does the first hour in the workshop look like?
I sweep. Everyone reads that as modesty; it is not. Sweeping tells me what I did yesterday — where the shavings fell, which machine ran, what I left half-clamped. By the time the floor is clear I know what the day is.
Which tool would you never replace?
A shoulder plane my father rehandled in 1974. Not because it is precious — the sole is worn hollow and I have to compensate for it — but because I know exactly how much it lies. A new one would be more accurate and I would trust it less.
When did something last fail?
March. A dining table, eight seats, and the top cupped like a roof tile three weeks after delivery. My glue-up, my humidity, my fault. I drove down, took it back and built a second one. The client got the better table. I got the lesson, which is the more expensive of the two.
